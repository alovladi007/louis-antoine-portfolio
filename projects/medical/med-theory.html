1. Medical Imaging Modalities

Different imaging modalities capture distinct tissue properties, requiring specialized preprocessing and model architectures.

CT (Computed Tomography)

CT uses X-ray attenuation measured in Hounsfield Units (HU):

  • Water: 0 HU, Air: -1000 HU, Bone: +1000 HU
  • Windowing essential for visualization (lung: -600 to 1500 HU)

MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

MRI signal intensity depends on tissue relaxation properties T1, T2, and proton density.

  • T1-weighted: Fat bright, fluid dark
  • T2-weighted: Fluid bright, useful for edema detection
  • FLAIR: CSF suppressed, lesions visible

Medical image augmentation must preserve anatomical validity:

  • Intensity: gamma correction, histogram matching
  • Noise: Gaussian noise, contrast adjustment
