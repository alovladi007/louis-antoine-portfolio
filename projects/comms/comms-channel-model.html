About Underwater Acoustic Channels

The underwater acoustic channel is one of the most challenging communication mediums. It exhibits severe multipath from surface/bottom reflections, frequency-dependent absorption (higher frequencies attenuate faster), Doppler spread from vehicle motion and water currents, and ambient noise from biological, shipping, and environmental sources. This simulator models these effects to predict link performance.

Underwater Acoustic Channel Theory

Transmission Loss

Total transmission loss combines spreading loss and frequency-dependent absorption:

TL(f, d) = 20 log₁₀(d) + α(f) × d / 1000

Where d is range in meters and α(f) is the absorption coefficient in dB/km.

Thorp's Absorption Formula

Empirical model for absorption in seawater (valid for 0.1-100 kHz):

α(f) = 0.11f² / (1 + f²) + 44f² / (4100 + f²) + 2.75×10⁻⁴f² + 0.003

The first term represents boric acid relaxation, the second is magnesium sulfate relaxation, and the remaining terms account for viscosity and other losses. Frequency f is in kHz.

Multipath Channel Model

The channel impulse response is the sum of delayed and attenuated copies:

h(t) = Σ aₙ δ(t - τₙ) e^(jφₙ)

Where:

  • aₙ = amplitude of path n
  • τₙ = delay of path n
  • φₙ = phase of path n

Ray Tracing for Path Delays

Path delay depends on geometry. For surface reflection with transmitter at depth d_tx, receiver at depth d_rx, and horizontal range r:

τ_surface = √(r² + (d_tx + d_rx)²) / c

For bottom reflection at depth H:

τ_bottom = √(r² + (2H - d_tx - d_rx)²) / c

Speed of sound c ≈ 1500 m/s in seawater.

Doppler Spread

Vehicle motion at speed v causes Doppler shift:

f_d = f_c × v / c

Multipath from different angles creates Doppler spread, limiting channel coherence time:

T_c ≈ 1 / (2π B_d)

Where B_d is the Doppler spread in Hz.

Coherence Bandwidth

The coherence bandwidth is inversely related to delay spread τ_rms:

B_c ≈ 1 / (2π τ_rms)

Signals with bandwidth larger than B_c experience frequency-selective fading and require equalization.
