About Channel Estimation

Channel estimation is critical for coherent underwater acoustic communication. Due to severe multipath and Doppler, the channel changes rapidly, requiring frequent pilot symbols and adaptive estimation algorithms. This tool compares Least Squares (LS), Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE), and adaptive LMS/RLS estimators.

Channel Estimation Theory

Least Squares (LS) Estimator

The LS estimator minimizes the squared error between received and expected pilot symbols:

H_LS = (X^H X)^(-1) X^H Y = X^(-1) Y

Simple but suffers from noise enhancement at low SNR.

MMSE Estimator

The MMSE estimator uses channel correlation knowledge to suppress noise:

H_MMSE = R_HH (R_HH + s^2(X^H X)^(-1))^(-1) H_LS

Optimal for known statistics but requires correlation matrix knowledge.

LMS Adaptive Filter

Updates estimates sample-by-sample using gradient descent:

h(n+1) = h(n) + mu * x*(n) * e(n)

Step size mu trades convergence speed for steady-state error.

RLS Adaptive Filter

Recursive least squares with exponential forgetting factor lambda:

h(n) = h(n-1) + k(n) * e(n), k(n) = P(n-1)x(n) / (lambda + x^H(n)P(n-1)x(n))

Faster convergence than LMS but higher complexity.
