About OFDM for Underwater Acoustics

OFDM divides the available bandwidth into multiple orthogonal subcarriers, each modulated independently. This approach provides excellent resistance to frequency-selective fading and Doppler spread in the challenging underwater acoustic channel. Typical underwater OFDM systems use 64-256 subcarriers spanning 8-16 kHz bandwidth with symbol durations of 5-20 ms.

OFDM Theory for Underwater Acoustics

Frequency-Selective Fading

The underwater acoustic channel suffers from severe frequency-selective fading due to multipath propagation. Sound reflects off the surface, bottom, and thermoclines, creating multiple delayed copies of the transmitted signal. This causes deep nulls in the frequency response at specific frequencies.

OFDM Symbol Structure

An OFDM symbol consists of N subcarriers spanning bandwidth B. The subcarrier spacing is:

Δf = B / N

The OFDM symbol duration Ts (excluding cyclic prefix) is the inverse of subcarrier spacing:

Ts = 1 / Δf = N / B

Cyclic Prefix

The cyclic prefix guards against inter-symbol interference (ISI) from multipath. It must be longer than the channel delay spread. With cyclic prefix length Tcp, the total symbol duration becomes:

Ttotal = Ts + Tcp

Data Rate Calculation

The raw data rate depends on subcarriers, modulation order M, and symbol rate:

Rraw = N × log2(M) × (1 / Ttotal)

After applying FEC with rate Rfec, the coded data rate becomes:

Rcoded = Rraw × Rfec

Underwater Acoustic Propagation Loss

Signal attenuation in seawater combines spreading loss and frequency-dependent absorption:

TL(f, d) = 20 log10(d) + α(f) × d / 1000

Where:

  • d = range in meters
  • α(f) = absorption coefficient in dB/km (Thorp's formula)
  • f = frequency in kHz

Thorp's empirical formula for absorption is:

α(f) = 0.11f² / (1 + f²) + 44f² / (4100 + f²) + 2.75×10⁻⁴f² + 0.003

Doppler Effect

Vehicle motion causes Doppler shifts that can destroy orthogonality between subcarriers. Maximum tolerable Doppler spread should be less than 1-2% of subcarrier spacing. This limits vehicle velocity or requires Doppler compensation.
