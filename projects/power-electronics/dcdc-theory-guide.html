DC-DC Converter Theory

Fundamental principles of switch-mode power supplies

Buck Converter Fundamentals

The synchronous buck converter steps down voltage using PWM control and an LC filter.

$$V_{out} = D \cdot V_{in}$$

where D is the duty cycle (0 to 1)

Continuous Conduction Mode (CCM)

Inductor current never reaches zero. Higher efficiency at full load.

Discontinuous Conduction Mode (DCM)

Current goes to zero each cycle. Simpler control but higher ripple.

Inductor Current Ripple

$$\Delta I_L = \frac{V_{in} - V_{out}}{L} \cdot \frac{D}{f_{sw}}$$

The inductor current ripple is inversely proportional to inductance and switching frequency. Typical designs target 20-40% ripple ratio (ΔIL/Iout).

Output Voltage Ripple

$$\Delta V_{out} \approx \frac{\Delta I_L}{8 \cdot f_{sw} \cdot C_{out}} + \Delta I_L \cdot ESR$$

Output ripple has two components: capacitive (from charge/discharge) and resistive (from ESR). Modern MLCCs have very low ESR, making capacitive ripple dominant.

Power Loss Analysis

Conduction Losses

$$P_{cond} = I_{out}^2 \cdot (R_{ds,on} + DCR)$$

Switching Losses

$$P_{sw} = \frac{1}{2} V_{in} \cdot I_{out} \cdot (t_r + t_f) \cdot f_{sw}$$

Gate Drive Losses

$$P_{gate} = Q_g \cdot V_{gate} \cdot f_{sw}$$

Control Loop Design

Voltage-mode control uses a compensator to achieve stable regulation with adequate phase and gain margins.

$$f_c < \frac{f_{sw}}{5} \text{ to } \frac{f_{sw}}{10}$$

Crossover frequency should be well below switching frequency

  • Gain Margin: > 6 dB for stability
  • Phase Margin: > 45° (typically 50-60°)
  • Type II/III compensator provides necessary phase boost

Digital Control

Digital PID implementation discretizes the continuous controller:

u[n] = Kp*e[n] + Ki*Ts*Σe[k] + Kd*(e[n]-e[n-1])/Ts

Key considerations: ADC resolution, PWM resolution, computation delay, and anti-windup for integrator.
