About Preston Equation

The Preston equation is the foundational model for CMP, relating material removal rate to applied pressure and relative velocity. Different materials have different Preston coefficients (Kp) reflecting their mechanical properties and chemical reactivity. Typical values: Oxide Kp = 1.2×10⁻⁷, Copper Kp = 2.5×10⁻⁷, Tungsten Kp = 1.8×10⁻⁷ cm²/dyne.

Preston Equation Theory

Fundamental Preston Equation

F.W. Preston proposed in 1927 that material removal rate is proportional to the product of pressure and velocity:

MRR = Kp × P × V

Where:

  • MRR = Material Removal Rate (nm/min or Å/min)
  • Kp = Preston coefficient (material and slurry dependent, typically 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁶ cm²/dyne)
  • P = Applied pressure (psi or Pa)
  • V = Relative sliding velocity (m/s or cm/s)

Physical Interpretation

The Preston equation captures the mechanical work done during polishing. Pressure determines contact force and asperity deformation, while velocity determines the rate of fresh surface exposure and slurry transport. The Preston coefficient encapsulates:

  • Material mechanical properties (hardness, elastic modulus)
  • Chemical reactivity (oxidation, hydrolysis rates)
  • Pad properties (hardness, roughness, porosity)
  • Slurry chemistry (pH, oxidizer concentration, abrasive type/size)

Extended Preston Model

Modern CMP often uses an extended Preston equation accounting for chemical effects:

MRR = Kmech × P × V + Kchem × C × exp(-Ea/RT)

The first term represents mechanical abrasion (classical Preston), while the second captures temperature and chemistry-dependent chemical dissolution. This is critical for copper CMP where oxidation dominates.

Pressure-Velocity Coupling

In real CMP, pressure and velocity are coupled through pad deformation. High pressure compresses the pad, reducing effective velocity at the contact interface. The modified velocity is:

Veff = Vnominal × (1 - P/Pcritical)

Unit Conversions

Common unit conversions for CMP calculations:

  • 1 psi = 6894.76 Pa = 68947.6 dyne/cm²
  • 1 m/s = 100 cm/s
  • 1 nm/min = 10 Å/min
  • Typical Kp in SI units: 1×10⁻¹³ to 1×10⁻¹² m²/Pa·s

Limitations of Preston Model

While widely used, the Preston equation has limitations:

  • Does not account for pattern density effects (dishing, erosion)
  • Assumes uniform contact - breaks down at feature scale
  • Ignores slurry particle concentration and transport
  • Preston coefficient varies with process conditions (non-constant)
  • Does not capture endpoint transients or selectivity changes

For these reasons, contact mechanics models (Greenwood-Williamson), tribochemical models, and multiscale simulations are often needed for accurate CMP prediction.
