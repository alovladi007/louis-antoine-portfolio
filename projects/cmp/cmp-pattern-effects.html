Understanding Pattern Effects

Pattern effects in CMP refer to variations in material removal rate (MRR) and topography that occur due to differences in local pattern density, feature size, and layout geometry. Dense patterns with narrow lines and spaces experience different polishing behavior compared to isolated features or wide open areas. This leads to phenomena such as dishing (concave deformation in metal lines), erosion (thinning of dielectric in dense patterns), and step height evolution. Understanding and controlling these effects is critical for achieving uniform planarization across complex integrated circuit layouts with varying feature densities and dimensions.

Theoretical Background

1. Pattern Density Effects

Pattern density is defined as the ratio of raised features (metal lines) to total area within a local region. It fundamentally affects the effective contact area between the wafer and polishing pad, which in turn influences the local material removal rate. High-density patterns experience more uniform pressure distribution but can lead to increased erosion, while low-density patterns may show reduced MRR and higher step heights.

ρ = W_line / (W_line + W_space)

where ρ is pattern density, W_line is line width, and W_space is space width.

2. Modified Preston Equation for Patterned Surfaces

The classical Preston equation must be modified to account for pattern density and local pressure variations. The effective removal rate depends on both the nominal applied pressure and the pressure enhancement factor due to pattern geometry.

MRR = K_p × P_eff × V × f(ρ)

where K_p is the Preston coefficient (material-dependent), P_eff is the effective pressure accounting for pattern effects, V is the relative velocity, and f(ρ) is a pattern density correction factor.

3. Dishing Mechanism

Dishing occurs when soft metal lines polish faster than the surrounding hard dielectric material, creating a concave depression. The dishing depth increases with line width and polish time, and is influenced by the hardness ratio between materials.

D = (MRR_metal - MRR_oxide) × t × (W_line / L_p)

where D is dishing depth, t is polish time, W_line is line width, and L_p is the planarization length characteristic of the pad.

4. Erosion in Dense Patterns

Erosion refers to the thinning of dielectric material in dense pattern regions relative to isolated features. It results from the enhanced pressure on raised features in high-density areas, causing accelerated removal of the surrounding dielectric.

E = MRR_oxide × t × [1 - exp(-ρ / ρ_c)]

where E is erosion depth, ρ is pattern density, and ρ_c is a critical density parameter related to pad mechanical properties.

5. Step Height Reduction

The evolution of step height during CMP follows an exponential decay influenced by pattern density and process parameters. Complete planarization occurs when the step height approaches zero.

h(t) = h_0 × exp(-t / τ)

where h(t) is step height at time t, h_0 is initial step height, and τ is the characteristic planarization time constant dependent on pad properties and pressure.

6. Planarization Length

The planarization length is a characteristic distance over which the pad bridges features and averages out topography. It depends on pad elastic modulus, thickness, and applied pressure.

L_p = [E_pad × t_pad³ / (12 × P)]^(1/4)

where E_pad is pad elastic modulus, t_pad is pad thickness, and P is applied pressure.

7. Pressure Distribution Model

The effective pressure on raised features in patterned regions can be significantly higher than the nominal applied pressure due to contact mechanics. This pressure enhancement is the root cause of pattern-dependent removal variations.

P_eff = P_nominal × [1 + α × (1 - ρ)]

where α is a pressure enhancement coefficient dependent on pad stiffness and pattern geometry.

8. Selectivity and Material Ratio

The selectivity between different materials (metal vs. dielectric) determines the relative removal rates and thus the magnitude of dishing and erosion. High selectivity can exacerbate pattern effects.

S = MRR_metal / MRR_oxide = K_p,metal / K_p,oxide

Typical selectivity values range from 2:1 to 10:1 depending on slurry chemistry and materials being polished.
