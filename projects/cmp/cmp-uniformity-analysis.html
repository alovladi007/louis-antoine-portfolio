Understanding CMP Uniformity

Within-wafer non-uniformity (WIWNU) is a critical metric in CMP that quantifies the variation in material removal across the wafer surface. Non-uniformity arises from radial pressure distributions, velocity gradients, temperature variations, slurry flow patterns, and edge effects. WIWNU is typically expressed as the standard deviation of thickness measurements divided by the mean, often reported as a percentage. Achieving low WIWNU (typically <3% for advanced processes) requires careful optimization of consumables, process parameters, and equipment design. This tool enables visualization and analysis of uniformity patterns to identify root causes and optimization opportunities.

Theoretical Background

1. Within-Wafer Non-Uniformity (WIWNU) Definition

WIWNU quantifies the variation in material removal or remaining thickness across the wafer. It is typically defined as the standard deviation of measurements divided by the mean, expressed as a percentage. Lower WIWNU values indicate better uniformity and process control.

WIWNU = (σ / μ) × 100%

where σ is standard deviation of removal and μ is mean removal. Typical targets are <3% for advanced nodes.

2. Pressure Distribution and Contact Mechanics

The pressure distribution between carrier and wafer is influenced by carrier membrane design, zone pressures, and wafer backside topography. Non-uniform pressure leads directly to non-uniform removal rates via the Preston equation. Modern multi-zone carriers enable independent control of center, mid, and edge pressures.

P(r) = P_center × [1 + α × (r/R)^n]

where r is radial position, R is wafer radius, α is edge bias factor, and n is profile exponent.

3. Velocity Gradient Effects

The relative velocity between wafer and pad varies with radial position due to rotational motion. Higher velocities at the wafer edge typically lead to higher removal rates. Velocity profiles depend on carrier and platen rotation speeds and sweep patterns.

V(r) = √[(ω_c × r)² + (ω_p × r)² + 2 × ω_c × ω_p × r² × cos(θ)]

where ω_c is carrier angular velocity, ω_p is platen velocity, and θ is relative angle.

4. Edge Effects and Retaining Ring

The wafer edge experiences different contact conditions due to the retaining ring, which prevents edge rollover but can cause local pressure perturbations. Edge exclusion (typically 3mm) is common in uniformity calculations to account for these effects.

5. Center-Fast vs Edge-Fast Profiles

Center-fast profiles show higher removal in the center, often due to higher center pressure or temperature. Edge-fast profiles result from velocity gradients or edge-enhanced slurry flow. Profile shape is tuned via pressure zoning and conditioning.

6. Pattern Density Effects on Uniformity

Local pattern density variations across the wafer lead to non-uniform removal even with uniform process conditions. High-density regions may experience erosion while low-density areas retain height, contributing to WIWNU.

7. Pad Conditioning Impact

Non-uniform pad conditioning leads to spatial variations in pad roughness and cut rate, which translate to wafer-level non-uniformity. Conditioner sweep range and dwell time distribution must be optimized for uniform pad regeneration.

8. Statistical Process Control

WIWNU is monitored using control charts (X-bar, R charts) to detect process drifts. Site-to-site correlations and wafer-to-wafer trends reveal systematic issues vs. random variation. Advanced analytics enable root cause identification.
