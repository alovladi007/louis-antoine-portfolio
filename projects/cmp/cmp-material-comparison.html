Understanding Material-Specific CMP

Different materials exhibit vastly different behavior during CMP due to variations in mechanical properties (hardness, elastic modulus), chemical reactivity with slurry components, and surface chemistry. Oxide polishing relies on chemical attack and soft abrasion, while metal CMP combines oxidation-dissolution mechanisms with mechanical removal. Understanding material-specific Preston coefficients, removal rate dependencies on pH and oxidizer concentration, and selectivity relationships is essential for developing robust multi-layer polishing processes. This tool enables side-by-side comparison of six common CMP materials to reveal trade-offs and optimization opportunities.

Theoretical Background

1. Material-Specific Preston Coefficients

The Preston coefficient quantifies the fundamental removal rate per unit pressure-velocity product for each material. It depends on material hardness, chemical reactivity, and slurry formulation. Softer materials and chemically reactive materials typically have higher Preston coefficients. Typical values range from 1×10⁻⁷ to 20×10⁻⁷ cm²/dyne.

K_p = MRR / (P × V)

where K_p is the Preston coefficient (material and slurry dependent), MRR is material removal rate, P is pressure, and V is velocity.

2. Oxide (SiO₂) CMP

Silicon dioxide polishing uses alkaline slurries (pH 10-11) containing colloidal silica or ceria particles. The mechanism involves chemical attack by hydroxyl ions to form silicate species, followed by mechanical abrasion. Oxide CMP is characterized by moderate removal rates (1000-3000 Å/min), excellent uniformity, and low defectivity.

SiO₂ + 2OH⁻ → SiO₃²⁻ + H₂O

3. Copper CMP

Copper polishing employs acidic slurries (pH 3-5) with oxidizers (H₂O₂, Fe³⁺) and complexing agents (glycine, citric acid). The process oxidizes Cu to Cu²⁺, which then forms soluble complexes. Copper CMP achieves high removal rates (3000-6000 Å/min) but requires careful control to minimize dishing and corrosion.

Cu + H₂O₂ + 2H⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2H₂O

4. Tungsten CMP

Tungsten polishing uses alkaline slurries with strong oxidizers (Fe(NO₃)₃, KIO₃) to convert tungsten to soluble tungstate. Alumina abrasives are commonly used. Tungsten CMP shows high removal rates (2500-5000 Å/min) and good selectivity to oxide. The process is chemically dominated with mechanical assistance.

W + 2OH⁻ + 3/2 H₂O₂ → WO₄²⁻ + 2H₂O

5. Aluminum CMP

Aluminum polishing faces challenges due to rapid native oxide formation. Slurries are typically alkaline with phosphoric or citric acid to dissolve the oxide layer while protecting underlying aluminum. Removal rates are moderate (1500-3000 Å/min) with careful pH control required.

Al₂O₃ + 2OH⁻ → 2AlO₂⁻ + H₂O

6. Poly-Silicon CMP

Polycrystalline silicon polishing uses similar slurries to oxide CMP but requires consideration of grain boundary effects. Alkaline silica slurries provide removal rates of 1500-3500 Å/min. Grain orientation and doping concentration can affect local removal rates, requiring optimized slurry selectivity.

7. Silicon Nitride (Si₃N₄) CMP

Silicon nitride is harder than oxide and chemically more resistant, requiring aggressive slurries. Phosphoric acid or alkaline slurries with ceria abrasives provide removal rates of 500-1500 Å/min. High selectivity of oxide-to-nitride (>20:1) is achievable for STI applications.

Si₃N₄ + 12OH⁻ → 3SiO₃²⁻ + 4NH₃ + 3H₂O

8. Selectivity and Multi-Material Stacks

Selectivity is the ratio of removal rates between two materials. High selectivity enables stopping on one material while removing another, critical for damascene and STI processes. Selectivity is tuned via slurry chemistry, pH, oxidizer concentration, and abrasive type.

S_A:B = MRR_A / MRR_B

Typical Cu:oxide selectivity is 50:1 to 100:1, while oxide:nitride selectivity is 20:1 to 50:1.

9. Process Window Analysis

Process windows define the range of pressure, velocity, and time that achieve target removal while meeting uniformity, defectivity, and remaining thickness specifications. Materials with wider process windows are more forgiving to equipment variations and consumable drift.
