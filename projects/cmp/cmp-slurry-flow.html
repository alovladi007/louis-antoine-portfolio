About Slurry Flow in CMP

Slurry flow governs particle transport to the wafer-pad interface. Key phenomena include: convective transport (bulk flow), shear-induced particle migration (creates concentration gradients), centrifugal effects (radial pumping), and bow wave formation ahead of the wafer edge. Typical flow rates: 100-300 mL/min with particle concentrations 1-5 wt%.

Slurry Flow Theory

Navier-Stokes Equations

Slurry flow in the wafer-pad gap is governed by the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

ρ(∂u/∂t + u·∇u) = -∇p + μ∇²u + fbody

Where:

  • ρ = slurry density (1.01-1.05 g/cm³)
  • u = velocity vector field
  • p = pressure field
  • μ = dynamic viscosity (function of shear rate for non-Newtonian slurries)
  • fbody = body forces (centrifugal, gravitational)

Reynolds Number

The Reynolds number characterizes the flow regime (laminar vs turbulent):

Re = ρVL/μ

For CMP: V ~ 0.5-2 m/s (tangential velocity), L ~ 20 μm (gap height), giving Re ~ 100-2000. Transition to turbulence occurs around Re ~ 1000 in confined geometries.

Shear-Induced Migration

Particles migrate from high to low shear regions, creating concentration gradients:

Jmigration = -Dshear∇γ̇

Where γ̇ is the shear rate and Dshear is the shear-induced diffusivity proportional to a²γ̇ (a = particle radius). This causes particles to concentrate at the center of the gap, affecting MRR distribution.

Particle Transport Equation

Particle concentration C evolves according to the advection-diffusion equation:

∂C/∂t + u·∇C = D∇²C + Smigration

Where D is the Brownian diffusivity (D = kT/(6πμa) ~ 10⁻¹² m²/s for 80 nm particles) and Smigration accounts for shear-induced migration.

Péclet Number

The Péclet number compares convective to diffusive transport:

Pe = VL/D

For typical CMP conditions: Pe ~ 10⁶-10⁸, indicating convection-dominated transport. Particles follow streamlines with minimal lateral diffusion.

Groove Flow Dynamics

Pad grooves act as slurry reservoirs and distribution channels. Groove-to-gap flow rate ratio:

Qgroove/Qgap = (hgroove/hgap)³

With hgroove ~ 500 μm and hgap ~ 20 μm, the ratio is ~15,000:1, showing grooves carry most of the flow while the gap relies on lateral transport.

Bow Wave Effect

The wafer edge creates a stagnation point and pressure buildup (bow wave), affecting slurry entry:

  • Bow wave height: Δh ~ V²/(2g) ~ 50-200 μm
  • Creates backflow at wafer edge
  • Reduces edge MRR by 5-15%
  • Can be mitigated by retaining ring design and flow rate optimization
