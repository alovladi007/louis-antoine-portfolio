Gaussian State Formalism

Continuous-variable quantum states are fully characterized by their first moments (displacement) and second moments (covariance matrix). Squeezed states have anisotropic Wigner functions with reduced variance in one quadrature.

Wigner Function (Quasi-Probability):
W(x,p) = (1/πℏ) · exp[-(x-⟨x⟩)²/σ_x² - (p-⟨p⟩)²/σ_p²]
Gaussian centered at (⟨x⟩, ⟨p⟩) with widths σ_x, σ_p
Covariance Matrix (2-Mode):
V = [⟨ΔX₁²⟩ ⟨ΔX₁ΔX₂⟩]
[⟨ΔX₁ΔX₂⟩ ⟨ΔX₂²⟩ ]
Vacuum: V = I (identity matrix)
Squeezing Operator:
Ŝ(r) = exp[r(â†² - â²)/2]
σ_x² = e^(-2r), σ_p² = e^(2r)
Uncertainty product: σ_x · σ_p = 1 (minimum)
Symplectic Eigenvalues:
ν± = √[(A±C)/2 ± √((A-C)²/4 + B²)]
Physical state: ν± ≥ 1 (Heisenberg limit)
Separable if ν+ · ν- ≥ 1
Purity & Entropy:
μ = 1/√det(V) (purity, 0 < μ ≤ 1)
S = -log₂(μ) (von Neumann entropy, bits)
Pure state: μ = 1, S = 0
