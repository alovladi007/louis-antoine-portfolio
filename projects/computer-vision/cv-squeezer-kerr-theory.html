Kerr Nonlinearity & Four-Wave Mixing

χ⁽³⁾-based degenerate parametric amplification in silicon nitride microring resonators

Physical Principles

The Kerr effect in SiN enables four-wave mixing (FWM) where two pump photons at ω_p annihilate to create signal (ω_s) and idler (ω_i) photons. In the degenerate case (signal = idler = probe), this produces quadrature squeezing below the shot noise limit.

Kerr Nonlinear Phase Shift:
φ_NL = γ · P · L_eff

γ = (2π · n₂) / (λ · A_eff)

where n₂ = 2.4×10⁻¹⁹ m²/W for SiN, A_eff = effective mode area
Degenerate FWM Hamiltonian:
Ĥ_FWM = ħ · χ⁽³⁾ · (â†_p² · â_s · â_i + h.c.)

For degenerate case: â_s = â_i = â_probe

→ Ĥ_FWM = ħ · g · (â†_p² · â_probe² + h.c.)
Cavity-Enhanced Squeezing Spectrum:
S(Ω) = 1 - η_esc · (4 · G · κ²) / [(κ + γ_int)² + Ω²]

G = γ · P_pump / (ħω · FSR) = parametric gain coefficient
κ = κ_ext + κ_int = total cavity decay rate
γ_int = internal loss rate, η_esc = κ_ext/κ = escape efficiency
Ω = frequency offset from resonance
Group Velocity Dispersion (GVD):
β₂ = (λ³ / 2πc²) · d²n/dλ²

Phase-matching condition: Δβ = β(ω_s) + β(ω_i) - 2β(ω_p) ≈ 0

For anomalous dispersion (β₂ < 0): phase-matching bandwidth increases
