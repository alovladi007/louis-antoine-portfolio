Ethics in Medical AI

Principles, frameworks, and best practices for responsible development and deployment of AI systems in healthcare settings

Beneficence

AI systems must demonstrably improve patient outcomes and clinical workflows

Non-Maleficence

Minimize harm through rigorous testing, uncertainty quantification, and fail-safes

Justice

Ensure equitable performance across all patient demographics and populations

Ethical AI Framework

1

Data Governance

Informed consent, data provenance tracking, and representative sampling

2

Fairness Metrics

Define and measure equalized odds, demographic parity, and calibration

3

Explainability

Implement interpretable models, attention visualization, and uncertainty estimation

4

Human Oversight

Radiologist-in-the-loop design with appropriate automation levels

5

Continuous Monitoring

Real-world performance tracking with drift detection and retraining triggers

Stakeholder Considerations

Patients

Right to know AI involvement, access to explanations, opt-out options

Clinicians

Training on AI limitations, liability clarity, workflow integration

Healthcare Systems

ROI vs equity tradeoffs, regulatory compliance, malpractice considerations

Developers

Responsible innovation, documentation standards, bias awareness training

Regulators

FDA guidelines, CE marking, post-market surveillance requirements

Society

Healthcare access equity, workforce impact, trust in medical AI

"The development of AI in healthcare must be guided not just by what we can do, but by what we should do. Every algorithm deployed affects real patients, real families, and real communities."

- WHO Guidance on Ethics & Governance of AI for Health, 2021
