Lithography Theory & Practice

1. Wave Optics and Imaging Theory

Optical lithography is fundamentally based on wave optics, where light behaves as an electromagnetic wave rather than particles. The imaging process in a lithography scanner involves the projection of a mask pattern onto a wafer using a lens system with high numerical aperture (NA).

Hopkins Partially Coherent Imaging

The aerial image formation in a partially coherent imaging system is described by the Hopkins formulation, which accounts for the extended source size and partial coherence effects:

I(x,y) = ∫∫ TCC(fx,fy; fx',fy') · M(fx,fy) · M*(fx',fy') · exp[i·2π·(fx-fx')x + i·2π·(fy-fy')y] dfx dfy dfx' dfy'

TCC = Transmission Cross Coefficient
M = Mask spectrum
* = Complex conjugate

Rayleigh Resolution Criterion

The minimum resolvable feature size (resolution) and depth of focus (DOF) are fundamental limits in optical lithography:

Resolution: R = k₁ · λ / NA
DOF: DOF = k₂ · λ / NA²

where:
λ = wavelength (193 nm for ArF, 13.5 nm for EUV)
NA = numerical aperture of projection lens
k₁ ≈ 0.25-0.5 (process factor)
k₂ ≈ 0.5 (DOF factor)

Abbe Theory of Image Formation

Ernst Abbe's theory explains image formation as a two-step process: (1) diffraction at the mask creating orders at the pupil, and (2) interference of these orders at the image plane.

2. Resolution Enhancement Techniques (RET)

To push resolution beyond the classical Rayleigh limit, various RET methods are employed:

Off-Axis Illumination (OAI)

  • Annular Illumination: Ring-shaped source that improves DOF for dense features
  • Quadrupole: Four-pole source optimized for horizontal/vertical lines
  • Dipole: Two-pole source for unidirectional patterns

Optical Proximity Correction (OPC)

OPC modifies mask geometries to compensate for optical proximity effects. Model-based OPC uses lithography simulation to iteratively correct edge placement errors:

EPE(n) = CD_target - CD_simulated(n)
Δx(n+1) = Δx(n) + α · EPE(n)

where α is the learning rate/aggressiveness factor

Phase Shift Masks (PSM)

PSM uses destructive interference to enhance contrast. Attenuated PSM (att-PSM) has ~6% transmission with 180° phase shift, while alternating PSM uses full transmission with alternating phase.

3. Photoresist Chemistry and Processing

Dill ABC Model

The Dill model describes resist exposure and bleaching:

I(z,t) = I₀ · exp(-∫₀ᶻ A(z',t) dz')
A(z,t) = A + B · M(z,t)
dM/dt = -C · I(z,t) · M(z,t)

A = base absorption
B = bleachable absorption
C = photochemical rate constant
M = photoactive compound concentration

Post-Exposure Bake (PEB)

PEB drives acid diffusion and catalytic deprotection. Acid diffusion is modeled by Fick's law:

∂[H⁺]/∂t = D · ∇²[H⁺] + k_gen · [PAG*] - k_loss · [H⁺]

D = diffusion coefficient (typically 10-20 nm²/s)
k_gen = acid generation rate
k_loss = acid loss rate (quenching/evaporation)

4. Metrology and Process Control

Critical Dimension (CD) Measurement

  • CD-SEM: High-resolution electron microscopy (precision ~0.5 nm)
  • Scatterometry: Optical diffraction-based CD measurement
  • AFM: Atomic force microscopy for sidewall profiling

Overlay Metrology

Overlay measures layer-to-layer alignment accuracy. Advanced overlay models decompose errors into components:

Δx(x,y) = Δx₀ + Tx·x + Ty·y + Rx·y + Ry·x + Σ(Wafer terms) + Σ(Process terms)

Translation: Tx, Ty
Rotation: Rx, Ry
Wafer terms: expansion, orthogonality
Process terms: reticle, chuck, etc.

Line Edge Roughness (LER)

LER is characterized by power spectral density (PSD):

PSD(f) = σ² · ξ / [1 + (2π·f·ξ)²]^((α+1)/2)

σ = RMS roughness (3σ specification)
ξ = correlation length
α = roughness exponent

5. Advanced Topics

EUV Lithography

Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography at 13.5 nm wavelength uses reflective optics and enables sub-10nm patterning with NA up to 0.55 (High-NA EUV).

Multi-Patterning

  • LELE: Litho-Etch-Litho-Etch (2 masks)
  • SADP: Self-Aligned Double Patterning (spacer-based)
  • SAQP: Self-Aligned Quadruple Patterning (2x SADP)

Computational Lithography

Source-Mask Optimization (SMO) co-optimizes illumination and mask for best process window. This involves solving a high-dimensional optimization problem with pattern fidelity and manufacturability constraints.

Key Takeaways

  • Lithography is limited by wave optics - resolution scales with λ/NA
  • RET techniques (OAI, OPC, PSM) enable sub-wavelength patterning
  • Resist chemistry and PEB critically impact final CD and LER
  • Metrology precision must be 3-5x better than specification
  • EUV and multi-patterning enable continued Moore's Law scaling
